A fingerprint wallet you can test without building one.

MONO is a card wallet that opens for your finger and for your phone. Its firmware runs on a thumb-sized nRF52 board — and, unmodified, in a C++ test suite, in a terminal, and in this page as WebAssembly.

Inside the firmware

On the air Nordic UART, line by line

    How it works

    One firmware. Four places it runs.

    The sketch is compiled, unmodified, against a layer of virtual hardware — a fingerprint sensor with fingers that have identities, an IMU that can be shaken, a servo, a radio, flash that survives a power cycle. Every mock records what the firmware did to it, so a test can assert that a tamper really drove the latch shut, not merely that it printed so.

    Proved on a virtual clock

    A 30-second lockout or a 60-second walk-away alarm costs nothing: delay() advances the clock. A recorder proves that across an alarm, a ring, an unlock and an enrolment, the main loop never once sleeps longer than 20 ms.

    The app meets the real firmware

    The app's session layer is tested in Node against the WebAssembly build: the browser's WebCrypto HMAC has to agree, byte for byte, with the firmware's own SHA-256, or a saved key cannot reconnect.

    Only your phone, only your finger

    A phone pairs once, with the owner's finger on the sensor to say yes, and proves itself on every connection with a fresh challenge. The key crosses the air once and never again.

    CAD

    Assembled before it is printed

    The works model is a parametric script. Before a single STL is trusted, every part and every component goes into a digital mock-up that checks 226 pairs of solids for clashes, pushes a USB-C plug home, slides the cards out through the mouth, and proves the LED, the buzzer and the latch each have a way out.

    Hover a part

    • 0 clashes across 22 solids and 226 pairs
    • 6.25 mm of USB-C engagement through the case
    • 54.6 mm mouth for 53.98 mm cards, with snap nubs
    • 100 % septum between the cards and the electronics
    • 4.3 mm of M2 thread in every boss
    • 17.56 mm thick for six cards, and N_CARDS_B really drives it

    Review

    Every bug found, reproduced, fixed — and pinned by a test

    The full write-up, with the reproduction that found each one, is in docs/REVIEW.md. A few:

    1. Any phone in range could open the wallet with one unauthenticated line. Now: owner-confirmed pairing and an HMAC challenge on every connection.
    2. Enrolling after a delete overwrote someone else's finger, because the next id was count + 1.
    3. An alarm could leave the radio beaconing every 20 ms forever, even after the phone came back.
    4. Tapping Disconnect set off the walk-away alarm; and a stranger connecting silenced it.
    5. The same knock raised the alarm or not depending on what the main loop was doing.
    6. The case's USB-C and latch slots were blind behind 0.5 mm of plastic, and the halves of the looks model could not close: its pin holes were 0.05 mm deep.
    7. The concept was drawn around 52 mm cards. A real 53.98 mm card cannot fit it, which is where the 0.76 mm walls came from.
    8. The app said "Open" whether or not the wallet had heard, and fired overlapping Bluetooth writes that Chrome rejects.
